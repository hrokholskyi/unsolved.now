slug: p-vs-np
name: "P vs NP"
title: "We don't know whether every problem whose answer is easy to check is also easy to solve"
field: mathematics
statement: "If a computer can quickly verify that a proposed solution to a problem is correct, can a computer also always quickly find that solution in the first place?"
first_posed: 1971
posed_by: "Stephen Cook (and, independently, Leonid Levin)"
prize: "Clay Millennium Prize — $1,000,000"
summary: "Checking a finished Sudoku takes seconds; solving one from scratch can take far longer — and nobody has proved that this gap is real. Thousands of practically vital problems, from routing trucks to folding proteins, share one shocking property: a fast method for any single one of them would crack them all. Nearly everyone believes P ≠ NP, that finding truly is harder than checking, yet half a century of effort has not produced a proof — and researchers have even proved theorems showing that whole categories of known proof techniques can never settle the question."
why_it_matters: "If P = NP with a practical algorithm, most modern encryption would collapse, while optimization, drug design, and even the search for mathematical proofs would become vastly easier — the world would change overnight. A proof that P ≠ NP would put the security of cryptography and the genuine hardness of thousands of everyday problems on solid ground for the first time."
progress:
  - "1971 — Stephen Cook formally poses the problem; Leonid Levin arrives at it independently in the USSR"
  - "1975 — Baker, Gill and Solovay prove the 'relativization' barrier: whole families of techniques cannot resolve the question"
  - "1993 — Razborov and Rudich formulate the 'natural proofs' barrier, ruling out another broad class of attacks"
  - "2008 — Aaronson and Wigderson show 'arithmetization' techniques are also insufficient"
